Research

The group studies how chemicals transform in the environment at the molecular level, and the environmental impacts of the products they form. We use that understanding to design and evaluate safer chemicals and new water treatment technologies. A mechanistic focus links our four research themes: we determine reaction mechanisms, kinetics, and transformation products directly, often in single particles and droplets and at interfaces, where chemistry differs sharply from that in bulk solution.

Atmospheric chemistry

Wildfire smoke chemistry, aerosol–cloud–climate interactions, and the multiphase chemistry of individual aerosol particles: how reactive uptake of trace gases alters particle phase and morphology, and how those changes feed back on chemical reactivity.

Photochemistry and transformation products

Photochemistry, photochemical production of reactive species, and transformation products in aqueous environmental systems, to predict how synthetic chemicals transform and persist in the environment.

Sustainable chemistry

Safer chemical design through an environmental chemistry lens: assessing the hazards of new chemicals and their transformation products before large-scale production.

Water treatment and reuse

Contaminant remediation chemistry, including technologies that destroy persistent organic contaminants through aerosol-accelerated chemistry.

Across all four themes, we ask what reactions occur, which reactants drive them, how fast they proceed, and what transformation products form. New method and instrument development, testing, and application cut across all four themes.

Approaches

Our main experimental and computational approaches include:

  • Single-particle and single-droplet techniques, including aerosol optical tweezers, droplet microfluidics, and single-particle analysis
  • High-resolution mass spectrometry coupled to liquid and gas chromatography, with nontarget analysis to identify transformation products and unknown contaminants
  • Atmospheric reaction chamber experiments with real-time chemical ionization mass spectrometry and aerosol instrumentation
  • Aqueous and atmospheric photochemistry, including solar simulators, photoreactors, and transient absorption spectroscopy
  • High-throughput reaction kinetics in well plates using laboratory automation
  • Computational modelling of chemical properties and environmental fate, with Trent University's Canadian Environmental Modelling Centre
  • Extensive use of Trent's Water Quality Centre, the most comprehensive mass spectrometry facility in Canada, including the first FT-ICR-MS for ultrahigh-resolution mass spectrometry

Group members build and adapt instruments rather than only operate them, and combine experiments with computational modelling across chemistry, environmental science, and engineering.

Trent’s environmental research strengths

Trent University is a public university of about 14,000 students in Peterborough, southern Ontario, near Oshawa and Toronto. It is known across Canada for its strength in environmental research & education. It offered Canada’s first bachelor’s degree in environmental science and studies, and the Trent School of the Environment is one of the largest and most established undergraduate environmental institutions in the country. Trent’s research plan names healthy and sustainable communities and environments as a priority, and freshwater science is a long-standing strength.

Research at Trent is concentrated in the same areas. Trent holds 12 federally funded research chairs, most in environmental fields, and hosts 22 environmental research institutes, centres, and groups. Trent ranks in the top five among Canadian undergraduate universities for publications in the natural sciences and engineering, and in climate change research (Research Infosource 2025). It also ranks third in Ontario among mid-size universities for faculty awards (Maclean’s 2027).

Trent’s Water Quality Centre describes itself as the most comprehensive mass spectrometry facility in Canada, with 13 mass spectrometers. These instruments complement the new CFI-funded laboratories and instruments for our group at Trent, and Fleming College’s Centre for Advancement of Water and Wastewater Technologies is in the same region. The Canadian Environmental Modelling Centre, Trent Research Farm, Trent Environmental Wind Tunnel, and upcoming Cleantech Commons are also valuable resources available to us at Trent. Trent is interdisciplinary by design. Its scale makes collaboration easy among chemists, physicists, environmental scientists, social scientists, biologists, and materials scientists, which is crucial for advancing environmental research and sustainable solutions.

Living in Peterborough

Peterborough is a mid-sized city on the Otonabee River in the Kawartha Lakes region, with 134 lakes nearby. The Symons Campus spans both banks of the river, part of the Trent–Severn Waterway, and the Rotary Greenway Trail links campus to downtown. Downtown Toronto and Toronto Pearson International Airport are each about 90 minutes away by car; Ottawa is three hours away and Montreal five. GO buses run from campus 12 times a day and connect with GO trains to Toronto’s Union Station at Oshawa, and FlixBus runs direct to Pearson. The cost of living, especially housing, is significantly lower than in Toronto, and lakes, trails, and provincial parks are close by.

Join us

New group members will join us at Trent University, where we move in January 2027. We are recruiting postdoctoral associates, a lab manager, MSc and PhD students, and undergraduate research students, with positions starting as early as February 2027.

What we look for in graduate students and postdocs

Much environmental chemistry centres on measuring composition, concentrations, exposure, or physical properties. That work is valuable. In this group, though, we seek scientists whose skills reach the next molecular-level questions: what reaction is occurring, which reactants drive it, how fast, and what products form? We also seek those who can engineer a process or device that controls the reaction, or that allows us to better observe and understand it. If your background is mainly measurement-focused but includes kinetic, mechanistic, or engineering work, tell us about those research experiences.

Postdoctoral associates

We seek at least two postdoctoral associates who want to understand how and why chemicals transform in the environment, and to use that understanding to design safer chemicals and better water treatment technologies. We welcome applicants from chemistry, chemical and environmental engineering, and related fields, including people new to environmental systems.

Requirements

Applicants need a PhD in chemistry, chemical engineering, environmental engineering, or a closely related field, completed within the last seven years or by the start date. Their experimental research should involve reaction kinetics, mechanisms, or transformation products; photochemistry; water treatment chemistry or process engineering; or the physical chemistry and reactivity of single particles, droplets, or interfaces. The research program is experimentally focused but also incorporates computational chemistry and automated science workflows.

Expertise we value

We value deep hands-on expertise in at least one of these areas:

  • Water treatment chemistry and engineering, including reactor design, scale-up, and process modelling
  • Droplet microfluidics, including device design, fabrication, and flow control, plus on-chip chemical analysis
  • Building or adapting instruments, such as single-particle traps, laser spectroscopy, or mass spectrometry inlets and ionization sources
  • High-resolution mass spectrometry with nontarget analysis to identify unknown chemicals and their transformation products in complex matrices
  • Time-resolved or interfacial laser spectroscopy, such as transient absorption spectroscopy
  • Computational chemistry applied to reaction mechanisms

Experience with aqueous environmental or atmospheric chemical systems, fellowship or grant writing, mentoring students, instrument development and testing, and quantitative data analysis and programming are all assets.

At least one position has a strong entrepreneurial component. We are advancing a patent-pending water treatment technology, based on aerosol-accelerated chemistry, from the laboratory toward commercialization. We seek a postdoc who wants to lead that technology development, in a position that could grow into a leading role in a planned startup company. Experience with reactor scale-up, pilot testing, techno-economic analysis, intellectual property, or industry partnerships is an asset.

Lab manager

The lab manager runs the day-to-day operation of the new laboratories and research group, and helps set them up during 2027. Duties include operating, maintaining, and developing methods on the group's liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry (LC-MS) systems and related instruments. The LC-MS systems include a triple quadrupole for quantitation and a high-resolution instrument. The lab manager also prepares samples, trains students and postdocs on the instruments and in laboratory safety, and manages purchasing, inventory, chemical safety, and waste, as well as data management.

Applicants need a BSc and at least two years of relevant hands-on experience, or an MSc, in analytical chemistry, environmental chemistry, or a related field. They need substantial hands-on experience with LC-MS/MS: method development, quantitation, quality assurance, troubleshooting, and routine maintenance. Sample preparation and extraction, GC-MS, nontarget analysis workflows, autosamplers and laboratory automation, a broader background in mass spectrometry or optical spectroscopy, and prior laboratory management or safety coordination are assets. Experience with SCIEX or comparable instrument platforms and software is also an asset.

Graduate students (MSc and PhD)

We welcome students with a strong background in chemistry, chemical engineering, or environmental engineering, including those from related fields such as environmental science, physics, materials science, or forensic science with substantial coursework in physical, analytical, or organic chemistry. Projects span how chemical reactions work in the environment, the safe design and evaluation of new chemicals and materials, new chemistries and reactors that destroy persistent contaminants and enable wastewater reuse, and the development of new methods, especially for capturing and analyzing single particles and droplets. We train students in the specialized instruments and methods the group uses, with a focus on mass spectrometry and optical spectroscopy. We offer competitive, fully funded packages for graduate students.

Students enroll through Trent's Environmental and Life Sciences or Materials Science graduate programs. Both programs can host most of our projects. Environmental and Life Sciences is the usual home for projects centred on chemistry in the environment: atmospheric aerosol and wildfire smoke chemistry, aerosol–cloud–climate interactions, photochemistry and transformation products, contaminant fate, chemical hazard assessment, and water treatment. Materials Science is the usual home for projects centred on particles, surfaces, and interfaces; the properties, characterization, and safe design and evaluation of materials; and devices and instruments, including ice nucleation, single-particle analysis, and droplet microfluidics. Materials Science requires a degree in chemistry, physics, or engineering, or equivalent qualifications, so it expects a deeper background in those subjects. Environmental and Life Sciences is interdisciplinary; to join our group through it, you need a four-year bachelor’s degree in chemistry, physics, environmental science, engineering, or a closely related STEM field, with substantial chemistry coursework. Many projects fit either program, and we will advise invited applicants on the best fit. Students begin in the MSc unless they already hold one, and MSc students can transfer to the PhD. Selected applicants are invited to a Zoom interview with Prof. Sullivan. Students offered a funding package after the interview then apply formally to the graduate program through Trent. A faculty member must agree to financially support each thesis-based MSc or PhD student, so please start with our application form below, not with a direct application to Trent. Trent's application deadline for September 2027 entry is 1 February 2027.

We also host co-op work terms for students in Trent’s Master of Instrumental Chemical Analysis program.

How to apply

  1. Complete our application form, which takes 20 to 30 minutes if you draft the longer answers in advance. Apply for one position only. The form asks for your CV as a PDF; three references; a self-rating on 17 techniques; about 200 words on a question in your first-choice research theme and how you would investigate it; about 250 words on why our group at Trent University is a good fit for your interests and skills; about 200 words on your career objectives and how joining our group at Trent University would advance them; and questions specific to the position:
    • Postdoctoral associates: up to three published or submitted papers with your own contribution to each, and about 300 words on a research question you answered or a process, device, or instrument you engineered
    • Lab manager: unofficial transcripts as PDFs, the instruments you have run independently, and one analytical method you developed
    • Graduate students: unofficial transcripts as PDFs, relevant courses with grades, and one research question you worked on at the molecular or mechanistic level
  2. We invite selected applicants to interview, and we contact references only for applicants who advance past the first round.
  3. Graduate students offered a funding package after the interview then apply formally to Trent University's graduate program by 1 February 2027 for September 2027 entry, with Ryan Sullivan as supervisor.

Your CV should list your degrees with dates and research supervisors; key courses you have taken that relate to our research themes and approaches; your research experience, with your own role and the techniques you used; publications and presentations with DOIs or links; awards and fellowships; any experience mentoring or teaching others; and related work experience outside academic research. There is no page limit and no single correct format. For structure and sample CVs for science students, see UBC’s guide to curricula vitae. Leave out photos and personal details such as age, marital status, or citizenship. You may use AI tools to check grammar or translate, but the ideas, experience, and results in your answers must be your own.

Please apply by 31 December 2026 for full consideration; review continues into 2027 until the positions are filled. The form is the only way to apply for graduate, postdoctoral, and lab manager positions; we cannot reply to individual emails about these positions.
