Research

The group studies how chemicals transform in the environment at the molecular level, and uses that understanding to design safer chemicals and new water treatment technologies. A mechanistic focus links our four research themes: we determine reaction mechanisms, kinetics, and transformation products directly, often in single particles and droplets and at interfaces, where chemistry differs sharply from that in bulk solution.

Atmospheric chemistry

Wildfire smoke chemistry, aerosol–cloud–climate interactions, and the multiphase chemistry of individual aerosol particles: how reactive uptake of trace gases alters particle phase and morphology, and how those changes feed back on chemical reactivity.

Photochemistry and transformation products

Aqueous photochemistry, photosensitization, and transformation products, to predict how synthetic chemicals transform and persist in the environment.

Sustainable chemistry

Safer chemical design: assessing the hazards of new chemicals and their transformation products before large-scale production.

Water treatment and reuse

Contaminant remediation chemistry, including technologies that destroy persistent organic contaminants through aerosol-accelerated chemistry.

Across all four themes, we ask what reactions occur, which reactants drive them, how fast they proceed, and what transformation products form.

Approaches

Our main experimental and computational approaches include:

  • Single-particle and single-droplet techniques, including aerosol optical tweezers, droplet microfluidics, and single-particle analysis
  • High-resolution mass spectrometry coupled to liquid and gas chromatography, with nontarget analysis to identify transformation products and unknown contaminants
  • Atmospheric reaction chamber experiments with real-time chemical ionization mass spectrometry and aerosol instrumentation
  • Aqueous and atmospheric photochemistry, including solar simulators, photoreactors, and transient absorption spectroscopy
  • High-throughput reaction kinetics in well plates using laboratory automation
  • Computational modelling of chemical properties and environmental fate, with Trent University's Canadian Environmental Modelling Centre
  • Extensive use of Trent's Water Quality Centre, the most comprehensive mass spectrometry facility in Canada, including the first FT-ICR-MS for ultrahigh-resolution mass spectrometry

Group members build and adapt instruments rather than only operate them, and combine experiments with computational modelling across chemistry, environmental science, and engineering.

Join us

New group members will join us at Trent University, where we move in January 2027. We are recruiting MSc and PhD students, postdoctoral associates, and a lab manager to start as early as February 2027.

Graduate students (MSc and PhD)

We welcome students from chemistry, physics, engineering, and environmental science who are curious about how reactions work, and we train students in the instruments and methods described above.

Students enroll through Trent's Environmental and Life Sciences or Materials Science graduate programs. Students begin in the MSc unless they already hold one, and MSc students can transfer to the PhD. Trent requires a confirmed supervisor before you apply to the graduate program, so start with our application form below. Trent's application deadline for September 2027 entry is February 1, 2027.

Postdoctoral associates

We seek at least two postdoctoral associates who want to understand how and why chemicals transform in the environment, and to use that understanding to design safer chemicals and better water treatment technologies. We welcome applicants from chemistry, chemical and environmental engineering, physical chemistry, and related fields, including people new to environmental systems.

Requirements

Applicants need a PhD completed within the last seven years, or by the start date. Their research should involve reaction kinetics, mechanisms, or transformation products; photochemistry; water treatment chemistry or process engineering; or the physical chemistry of single particles, droplets, or interfaces.

Expertise we value

We value deep hands-on expertise in at least one of these areas:

  • Water treatment chemistry and engineering, including reactor design, scale-up, and process modelling
  • Droplet microfluidics, including device design, fabrication, and flow control
  • Building or adapting instruments, such as particle traps, laser spectroscopy, or mass spectrometry inlets and ionization sources
  • High-resolution mass spectrometry with nontarget analysis to identify unknown products
  • Time-resolved or interfacial spectroscopy
  • Computational chemistry applied to reaction mechanisms

Experience with both aqueous and atmospheric systems, fellowship or grant writing, mentoring students, and quantitative data analysis and programming are all assets.

What we look for

Much environmental chemistry centres on measuring composition, concentrations, or physical properties. That work is valuable. In this group, though, we want people whose skills reach the next question: what reaction is occurring, which reactants drive it, how fast, and what products form, or how to engineer a process or device that controls it. If your background is mainly measurement-focused but includes kinetic, mechanistic, or engineering work, tell us about that part.

The start date is as early as February 2027. These are existing vacancies.

Lab manager

The lab manager runs the day-to-day operation of a new laboratory and helps set it up during 2027. Duties include operating, maintaining, and developing methods on the group's liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry (LC-MS) systems, which include a triple quadrupole for quantitation and a high-resolution instrument. The lab manager also prepares samples, trains students and postdocs on the instruments and in laboratory safety, and manages purchasing, inventory, chemical safety, and waste. The role supports instrument installation and data management.

This is a staff position for applicants with a BSc and at least two years of relevant hands-on experience, or an MSc, in chemistry, environmental chemistry, or a related field. Applicants need substantial hands-on experience with LC-MS/MS: method development, quantitation, quality assurance, troubleshooting, and routine maintenance. GC-MS, nontarget analysis workflows, autosamplers and laboratory automation, and prior laboratory management or safety coordination are assets. SCIEX instruments and software are also an asset.

The start date is as early as February 2027. This is an existing vacancy.

How to apply

  1. Complete our application form and upload your CV as a PDF. The form takes about 20 minutes. It asks about your research experience, the techniques you have used, and one research question you worked on at the molecular level.
  2. We invite selected applicants to submit a full application and to interview. We contact referees only at this stage.
  3. Graduate students we accept then apply to Trent University's graduate program, with Ryan Sullivan as supervisor.

We review applications on a rolling basis, beginning in December 2026, until the positions are filled. The form is the only way to apply; we cannot reply to individual emails about these positions.
